# Kase 0.8.2

**Released:** 2026-10-30 · **Type:** patch release · **Breaking changes:** none

Faster battery while away, one switch away. Kase can read your AirPods' battery even when they aren't connected to the PC, for example when you open the case nearby. How quickly it sees those readings depends on a Bluetooth feature that Linux still labels experimental. This release adds a switch to turn it on, with the same care as Apple device mode.

## Highlights

- **Faster battery while away.** A new switch in **Settings → Diagnostics** turns on Bluetooth's experimental features, so Kase can watch for your AirPods with the advertisement monitor instead of falling back to plain scanning.

## What's new

### Bluetooth

- **Faster battery while away** (off by default). Flip the switch, confirm, and enter your password. Bluetooth restarts (your devices drop for a few seconds), and within about a minute "Battery while away" shows **Advertisement monitor**. No need to restart Kase. ([#39])
- **Shows the truth.** The switch reflects what the running Bluetooth service actually does, not just what a settings file says.
- **Careful with your settings.** Kase keeps a one-time backup (`/etc/bluetooth/main.conf.bak-kase-experimental`) and adds a single `Experimental = true` line. Turning it off removes only that line. It never touches Apple device mode's lines, and the two switches work independently. ([#39])
- **Works across distributions,** like Apple device mode: on NixOS you get the `hardware.bluetooth.settings.General.Experimental = true;` line to add; systems without systemd restart Bluetooth through OpenRC or runit; SteamOS explains that the account needs a password first; and where Kase can't ask for your password, it gives you the terminal command to copy. ([#39])

## Should I turn it on?

If the case or pod levels feel slow to update when the AirPods aren't connected, yes. If not, there's no need. Everything else in Kase works the same either way. If you notice anything odd with your Bluetooth devices afterwards, turn it off again with the same switch.

## Upgrading

Open a terminal and run:

```bash
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DaBarkle/kase/main/linux-rust/packaging/install.sh | bash
```

Kase restarts in the tray with your settings, saved AirPods and Flatpak overrides intact. The new switch starts off; your Bluetooth settings aren't changed until you flip it.

## Known limitations

- The switch changes a system-wide Bluetooth setting, so it affects every app that uses Bluetooth, not just Kase. BlueZ's experimental features are widely used, but they are labelled experimental by the Linux Bluetooth project.
- Everything else listed for [0.8.0](https://github.com/DaBarkle/kase/releases/tag/v0.8.0) and [0.8.1](https://github.com/DaBarkle/kase/releases/tag/v0.8.1) still applies.
